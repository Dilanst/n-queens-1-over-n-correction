\section{Scope of interval arithmetic}\label{sec:numcert}
What width can be guaranteed for $\cerr$ when grid estimates are replaced by rigorous bounds? The
first error that propagates to all the rows is the profile error. Solving its equations in floating
point gives a candidate, and the error of the first candidate dominated the test of the explicit
rows. For the new candidate a piecewise polynomial with rational coefficients, of degree 14 and
with 16 panels per unit, was used. Its residual in the equations was enclosed over the entire
domain. Coercivity in logarithmic variables and identification with the physical solution convert
this residual into uniform radii of $2.086\,10^{-15}$ for the axial factor and $4.248\,10^{-16}$
for the vacancy [O]. Each row must use the radii of the candidate it evaluates.

\paragraph{From the profile to the nodal values [O].}
The 43 distinct nodes in the calculation comprise 26 real Gauss nodes and 17 representatives of the
32-point circle. Enclosures were obtained for $G_{\rm cof}, f_{4,\rm expl}, f_{2F}$ and the
moment $T_1$ at all 43. The square contribution $C_{3,\rm sq}$ also has enclosures at these points.
On the real axis, nested moments and the explicit part of $\mathcal V$ were also enclosed. Norm
bounds were obtained for the two coincidences $\Delta_1,\Delta_2$. Reaching the tolerances used by
the quadrature also requires controlling these two contributions and the rows containing the
resolvent with sufficient accuracy.

\paragraph{Boundary values [O].}
For $S$, symmetry and subdivision at the folds of the profile reduce the integral to 27 tetrahedra.
Polynomial envelopes, verified Gauss nodes, an analytic remainder, and outward rounding~\cite{mkc}
give
\[
 15.6334582797746\le S\le 15.6334582811044 .
\]
For $P_T$, interval products enclose the powers of the centered rook kernel and its measure
variation. Folding by reflection, with 2048 cells per half-axis and powers up to order 14, gives
\[
 0.444317034618897\le P_T\le 0.446141238271620 .
\]
The final cubature for $S$ took 25.13 CPU seconds. The 2048-cell calculation of $P_T$ took 111.75
CPU seconds on one thread. Its half-width is $0.0009121018263615$. For the boundary term $J$, the
signed coefficients remain to be integrated jointly over the position and the availability state of
the pair.

\paragraph{What limits the enclosure of $Q_2$.}
To study one of the rows containing the resolvent, node 10 of the 20-point panel was chosen. Its
quadrature weight divided by $x^2$ is approximately $0.294$. Even allocating the entire target of
$10^{-4}$ to this node, the half-width of $Q_2$ would have to be about $3.4\,10^{-4}$.
The integration retained both diagonal parities and the sign of the 17 trace contributions after
cancellation. The starting point was a certified model of $L_0$ with 256 panels per unit. For
integration they were grouped into 16 panels per unit and 512 centers were used. The full continuum
integral lay in $[-49913.764054;50371.535812]$ [O], with a half-width of about $5.0\,10^4$.
The calculation took 51.27 CPU seconds.

Would it suffice to ungroup the panels? In the trace $S_{13}$, the two axial orientations enter
with the same sign. Each contains two terms from the row--column block of $L_0$, with coefficient
one. The smallest width among the $65536$ entries of that block was $w=108349/131072$. Both axial
densities have mass one. After multiplication by the prefactor $1/4$, separate interval evaluation
of this trace leaves a half-width of at least $w/2>0.41$, even after ungrouping all the original
panels. The minimum in the table is certified [O]. The lower bound [D] holds for that table when
$S_{13}$ is evaluated separately. The identities of the physical kernel may link its entries more
tightly.

Combining the global bounds gives $[-4.28\,10^{19};4.28\,10^{19}]$ [O]. The interval includes
zero and leaves both the sign and the decimal digits of $\cerr$ open. Obtaining a tight enclosure
by the rectangle method would require a proven and sufficiently small bound on the spatial remainder
in the physical grid classes. This would justify the spatial tolerances at the nodes on which the
bound for integration in $x$ remains conditional.
