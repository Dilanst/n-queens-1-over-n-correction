\section{Scope of interval arithmetic}\label{sec:numcert}
What width can we guarantee for $\cerr$ when we replace the grid estimates with rigorous bounds?
The first error that propagates to all rows is the profile error. Solving the profile equations in
floating-point arithmetic gives a candidate, and the error in the first candidate dominated the
trial for the explicit rows. For the new candidate we used a piecewise polynomial of degree 14
with rational coefficients and 16 panels per unit. We enclosed its residual in the equations over
the entire domain. Coercivity in logarithmic variables and identification with the physical
solution turn this residual into uniform radii of $2.086\cdot10^{-15}$ for the axial factor and
$4.248\cdot10^{-16}$ for the vacancy \status{O}. Each row must use the radii of the candidate it
evaluates.

\paragraph{From the profile to nodal values \status{O}.}
The 43 distinct nodes in the calculation comprise 26 real Gauss nodes and 17 representatives of
the 32-point circle. We enclosed $G_{\rm cof}, f_{4,\rm expl}, f_{2F}$ and the moment $T_1$ at all
43 nodes. The square contribution $C_{3,\rm sq}$ also has enclosures at these points. On the
real axis we also enclosed nested moments and the explicit part of $\mathcal V$. For the two
coincidences $\Delta_1,\Delta_2$ we obtained norm bounds. Meeting the tolerances used by the
quadrature also requires controlling these two contributions and the rows containing the resolvent
with sufficient precision.

\paragraph{Boundary values \status{O}.}\label{par:intervalos-frontera}
For $S$, symmetry and subdivision along the folds of the profile reduce the integral to 27
tetrahedra. Polynomial envelopes, verified Gauss nodes, an analytic remainder and outward
rounding~\cite{mkc,tucker} give
\[
 15.6334582797746\le S\le 15.6334582811044 .
\]
For $P_T$, interval products enclose the powers of the centered rook kernel and their variation
with respect to the measure. Folding by reflection, with 2048 cells per half-axis and powers up to
order 14, gives
\[
 0.444317034618897\le P_T\le 0.446141238271620 .
\]
The final cubature for $S$ used 25.13 CPU seconds. The 2048-cell calculation of $P_T$ used 111.75
CPU seconds on one thread. Its half-width is $0.0009121018263615$. For the boundary term $J$, the
signed coefficients still have to be integrated jointly over the position and availability state
of the pair.

\paragraph{What limits the enclosure for $Q_2$.}
To study one of the rows containing the resolvent, we selected node 10 of the 20-point panel. Its
quadrature weight divided by $x^2$ is approximately $0.294$. Even if the entire target of
$10^{-4}$ were allocated to that node, the half-width for $Q_2$ would have to be about
$3.4\cdot10^{-4}$. The integration retained both diagonal parities and the signs of the 17 trace
contributions after cancellation. We started from a certified model of $L_0$ with 256 panels per
unit. For integration we grouped them into 16 panels per unit and used 512 centers. The full
continuum integral was enclosed in $[-49913.764054;50371.535812]$ \status{O}, with a half-width of about
$5.0\cdot10^4$. The calculation used 51.27 CPU seconds.

Would ungrouping the panels suffice? In the trace $S_{13}$, the two axial orientations enter with
the same sign. Each contains two terms from the row--column block of $L_0$, with coefficient one.
The smallest width among the $65536$ entries in this block was $w=108349/131072$. Both axial
densities have mass one. After multiplication by the prefactor $1/4$, a separate interval
evaluation of this trace leaves a half-width of at least $w/2>0.41$, even if all the original
panels are ungrouped. The minimum in the table is certified \status{O}. The lower bound \status{D}
applies to
that table when $S_{13}$ is evaluated separately. The identities for the physical kernel may
constrain its entries more tightly.

\paragraph{Overall error budget.}\label{par:balance-global}
We did not obtain a useful enclosure for $\cerr$. The absolute bounds for the integral of
$\numerr_6/x^2$ dominate, with additional bounds for the pair coefficients and the other boundary
terms. Certifying the sign and decimal digits by the rectangle method requires a proven,
sufficiently small bound on the spatial remainder for the physical grid classes. Such a bound
could justify the sample tolerances required by the integration bound for $\numerr_6/x^2$.
