\section{Finite counts and quadrature bounds}\label{app:numquad}
\subsection{Comparison with finite boards}

whose limit the theorem gives. For $10\le n\le27$, the table shows the integer $Q(n)$ and the
scaled residual obtained with the canonical predictor. This last column depends on a numerical
calculation and is rounded to six decimal places. The data file in the supplement records
$\log Q_{\rm pred}(n)$ and the residual for each size. Figure~\ref{fig:counts} shows the last
twelve values.

To see how much of the finite-size variation a period-four oscillation explains, the archived
fit used the sizes $16\le n\le27$. It fixed $\cerr=0.4055004460$, the estimate then available
from the continuum, and fitted the other three parameters of the model
\[
 nR_Q(n)=\cerr+\frac{a}{n}+A e^{-\kappa(n-16)}\cos(\pi n/2)+\text{residual}.
\]
The fit gave $a=-2.956$, $\kappa=0.166$, and $A=0.297$ [N]. Including the oscillation reduced
the root mean square error from $0.128$ to $0.041$. The correction $a/n$ with $a$ close to
$-2.9$ and the oscillation are appreciable at these sizes. The counts are consistent with the
computed value of $\cerr$, but the table alone cannot determine it. The two-cut-off argument
establishes the limit independently of this fit.
