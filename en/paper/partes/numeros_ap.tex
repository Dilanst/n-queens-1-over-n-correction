\section{Finite counts and quadrature bounds}\label{app:numquad}
\subsection{Comparison with finite boards}\label{sec:ajuste-finito}
The exact counts, tabulated in~\cite{oeis} up to $n=27$~\cite{q27}, let us observe the quantity
whose limit the theorem gives. For $10\le n\le27$, the table gives the integer $Q(n)$ and the
scaled residual obtained with the canonical predictor. The last column depends on a numerical
calculation and is rounded to six decimal places. The supplement's data file records $\log Q_{\rm
pred}(n)$ and the residual for each size. Figure~\ref{fig:counts} shows the last twelve values.

To see how much of the finite-size variation a period-four oscillation explains, the archived fit
used the sizes $16\le n\le27$. It fixed $\cerr=0.4055004460$, the continuum estimate at the time.
We retain this value to reproduce the fit and Figure~\ref{fig:counts} using
\path{suplemento/datos/ajuste_finito.json}. It differs by less than $7\cdot10^{-8}$ from the
updated center $0.40550038$ and falls within the conservative band. The other three parameters
were fitted to the model
\[
 nR_Q(n)=\cerr+\frac{a}{n}+A e^{-\kappa(n-16)}\cos(\pi n/2)+\text{residual}.
\]
The cosine is zero for odd $n$, $+1$ for $n\equiv0\;(\mathrm{mod}\,4)$, and $-1$ for
$n\equiv2\;(\mathrm{mod}\,4)$. The periodic contrast is between the two even residue classes. The
fit gave $a=-2.956$, $\kappa=0.166$ and $A=0.297$ [N]. Including the oscillation reduced the root
mean square error from $0.128$ to $0.041$. Both the correction $a/n$ with $a$ close to $-2.9$ and
the oscillation are appreciable at these sizes. The counts are consistent with the calculated
value of $\cerr$, but the table alone does not determine it. The two-cut argument establishes the
limit independently of this fit.
